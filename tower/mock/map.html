Opticon mock map

Bentham's own Panopticon, drawn to the sizes in his 1787 letters: a round building 100 feet across, an inspector's lodge 35 feet across in the middle, an open ring of floor 14 feet wide, and one ring of 48 cells, each 13 feet deep. The faint squares are the game's board, three feet to a square. The inspector is in the west quarter tonight, shaded red. You are the white dot, in your cell.

Quiet way, solid line

Pick your cell gate, cross the open area round to the passage, and walk out into the yard. Silent, but the area is always in view of whichever quarter the inspector is in.

Loud way, dotted line

Break your window and drop into the yard. Short, and nothing inside sees you go, but the whole building hears the glass.

  • Lodge

    The inspector sits in one of four quarters, behind blinds, so you never know which.

  • The quarter in view

    Everything here is seen, cell and area alike. There is no cover in it.

  • Grating and cell gate

    Bars face the lodge and hide nothing. The gate is locked: a pick opens it quietly.

  • Partition

    Solid, between cells, a few feet past the bars, so prisoners never see each other.

  • Window

    In the outer wall, lighting you from behind. Break it and you are out, loudly.

  • Passage

    The one way in or out, a cell wide, from the yard to the area.

Bentham's: the round plan, the lodge in quarters with blinds and lamps outside its windows, the area, the barred cells with outer windows, the partitions, the single passage. The game's: the yard, its wall and gate, and the routes. He also drew a second storey of cells reached by a gallery, left out here.
